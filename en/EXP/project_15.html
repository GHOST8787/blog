Internship at a Penghu Aquaculture Seedling Hatchery:
The Full Record of Raising Swimming-Crab Seedlings and Releasing 14,531

Engineering Aquaculture Marine Biology
2023.07 — 2023.08 BY GHOST_1010

Summer internship in the second year (spring semester) of the Department of Aquaculture, National Taiwan Ocean University, held at the Penghu County Aquaculture Seedling Hatchery (a government institution). I was assigned to the snail-and-crab group, working with the blue swimming crab Portunus pelagicus. During the internship I followed the cycle from fertilized egg to juvenile-crab release, a complete 12-day period, releasing a total of 14,531 individuals into the seagrass beds of Penghu's waters. The campus final report was completed in December 2023.

# Internship Tasks and Species

  • Institution — Penghu County Aquaculture Seedling Hatchery
  • Department — National Taiwan Ocean University, Department of Aquaculture
  • Group — Snail-and-crab group
  • Species — Blue swimming crab Portunus pelagicus (family Portunidae, common names sand crab / blue swimmer crab)
  • Habitat — Indian and western Pacific waters, intertidal zone to 50 m depth, sandy-mud bottom, with a sand-burrowing habit
  • Spawning traits — sperm overwinters inside the female; the crabs migrate to deeper water for mating and overwintering

# Microscopy: Three Stages Compared at 40x

During rearing I took samples from ponds A3, A4, and A6 each day, tracking metamorphic progress under 40x microscopy. The recorded shots span Day1 Zoea to Day12 juvenile crab. Three comparison images show the morphological change in one species across 12 days:

The A6 pond Day12 sample captured three views of the same individual transitioning from megalopa to juvenile crab (abdomen / dorsum / carapace), the most complete metamorphic record of this rearing period. At the Zoea stage I judged individual survival by heartbeat and activity, and video recording added the dynamic information that still images cannot show.

# Release: 8/3 A5 Outdoor Pond, 14,531 Individuals

On August 3 the juvenile crabs in the A5 outdoor pond reached release size, and the whole-pond operation ran in three steps:

  • Step 1 — Counting: count samples from pond A and pond B separately, then scale the sampling ratio back up to the whole-pond total.
  • Step 2 — Boxing: control the water temperature, add a buffering medium against collisions, and transport from the hatchery to the release site.
  • Step 3 — Release: choose a seagrass-bed area and release the crabs spread out along the seagrass clumps, giving the juveniles immediately usable shelter structure.

The counting calculation (from that day's release record): 

Pond A: 459 × 19.5 ≈ 8,950.5
Pond B: 279 × 20   ≈ 5,580
Total:             ≈ 14,531 individuals
