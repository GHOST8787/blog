After pushing train to 95.5%, why I submitted the zero-overfit 91%

AI & Automation Datasheet Extraction Schema Rule
2026.05.10 — 2026.05.22 BY GHOST_1010

The previous post, P13 NTUST AI Final Two-Axis Experiment, ended at V2's 76.4%. This one picks up from there: after submitting V2 I ran V3 through V48, 46 versions in total, and pushed train all the way to 95.5%. Late in that run I stopped and ran an overfit audit, and the conclusion flipped: the score-chasing rules from V37 onward all overfit a scoring set of "only 10 datasheets". So the final submission was a zero-overfit integrated version, 91 cells out of 100 (91%) on the 100-cell basis. I let go of the version that pushed train to 95.5%. This post writes out the full story of how the 46 versions changed, how 95.5% came about, and why I left it on the table.

First, the task and the scoring

The task: given 10 electronic component PDFs, extract 11 fields from each (part number, operating temperature, length/width/height, pin count, current, voltage, etc.), then compare cell by cell against the teacher's answer key specbook.xlsx to count how many cells are correct.

Why the denominator goes from 110 to 100: 11 columns × 10 datasheets = 110 cells, but among them the "part number" is given directly as input by the task (the filename maps to the part number, so the AI does not extract it), and it should not count toward the AI's score. Remove those 10 cells, and what the AI actually has to extract and be scored on is 10 columns × 10 datasheets = 100 cells. The submitted report uses this 100-cell basis.

So read the numbers in this post this way: the progress of each intermediate version uses the 110-cell figures recorded at the time (including the part number) as a process reference; the conclusion always reads the 100-cell figure. The final submission was a "zero-overfit" integrated version, 91 cells out of 100 (91%) — the version that peaked highest along the way I left behind. Below is the full process of how I worked up to that high point and why I did not adopt it in the end.

# What overfit is, and how I judge it

Every version in this post asks "does this version overfit", so let me explain overfit clearly up front.

Plain definition

Overfit means "a rule that only works for the 10 parts in front of you, and fails on an 11th new PDF". An extreme example: writing in the code "for part BAV99W, force V_RRM = 150" — this rule earns +1 against the train-set answer, but it does nothing for any unseen PDF. That is the essence of overfit.

There are two complementary standards for judging overfit. The first looks at what the rule itself is written to do:

  • Look at the rule content — a rule that names a specific part number (BAV99W, DFLS160, and the like) or hardcodes a number from the answer key = overfit. A rule that triggers only on ratios, patterns, or text patterns = generic.
  • Look at the rule source — a rule derived from industrial packaging convention, the JEDEC spec, or statistical properties of the specbook = generic. A rule derived from "checking the train-set answers to back out which question to apply it to" = overfit.
  • Look at the trigger condition — a trigger condition like "the ratio falls in some range" or "the PDF has some keyword", which applies consistently to any PDF, = generic. A trigger that says "apply to these 7 parts, do not apply to the other 3" = overfit.

One premise is key: this assignment's scoring set has only 10 datasheets, with no separate independent test set, so the batch used to tune rules is the same as the batch used to score. When those two batches are the same, any action of "checking which cells of these 10 parts are wrong and tuning the rule threshold back" raises the score, but what rises is overfit to those 10 datasheets. So in this post, the way I judge each version is the standard "what the rule itself is written to do, and where the threshold comes from".

# V8 — V11: multimodal with keyword page filtering, climbing to 84.5%

V8 starts combining multimodal with "use keywords first to filter out the most relevant pages in the PDF, and only send those pages to the vision LLM". A datasheet is usually 8-20 pages, but the information actually needed to answer the 11 fields concentrates in 2-3 pages (Package Outline, Electrical Characteristics). Dropping the irrelevant pages concentrates vision attention on the right place.

V11 pushed to 84.5% and stalled there. 84.5% equals 93 of 110 cells correct, and the remaining 17 errors all concentrate in the three "mechanical dimension L / W / H" columns. A long stretch afterward was wrestling with these 17 errors.

# V12 — V19: pure prompt to the wall

With all 17 errors in dimension columns, the first instinct is "the prompt does not spell out clearly enough how vision should read the Package Outline table". From V12 to V19 I worked the problem entirely by tuning the prompt, and finally found the ceiling of pure prompt engineering sits at 84.5%.

Version What changed Train
V12 in-context examples enter: put example answers for three packages (TO-247 / DPAK / D2PAK) in the prompt as guidance + 4 counterexamples 84.5%
V13 Raise counterexamples to 5-8, hoping to cover more common errors 81.8%
V14 multimodal critic: a second round where the LLM looks at the image again and verifies its own first-round answer 84.5%
V15 strict critic: the critic uses a stricter judgment standard 84.5%
V16 hybrid critic: a mix of strict + lenient two-path critics 81.8%
V17 Raise sampling temperature from 0 to 0.5, giving the LLM some randomness to see if it jumps out of a local optimum 84.5%
V18 Swap model: from GPT-4o to Llama-4-Maverick 84.5%
V19 ensemble critic: for the cells where V12 (GPT-4o) and V18 (Llama) disagree, run a GPT-4o multimodal critic once more to decide from the image 83.6%

V12, V14, V15, V17, V18, five versions all landed exactly on 84.5%. Swapping prompt, swapping critic, swapping sampling, swapping model none of them broke through.

An interesting detail: V12 (GPT-4o) and V18 (Llama) both took 84.5%, but the cells they got right differ. V18 got the L / W / H of some small packages (for example CD4148WTP) right on all three axes, while V12 got all three axes wrong. In other words, the two models each have their strengths, and there is no such thing as "one model is overall stronger". This observation is the starting point for the later multi-model voting.

Why this section could not get past it even with this prompting

The essence of pure prompt engineering is "show the LLM a paragraph of text description, add a few examples, and hope it learns how to read the image". But the vision model's reading bias on certain dimensions is at the model architecture level, rooted in the architecture itself — however you prompt it, it skews on small packages at the 1-2 mm scale. Solving this means stepping out of the prompt level and working at the layer of "what to do after the answer is generated".

# V20 — V28: multi-model voting, from 84.5% to 88.2%

Since V12 (GPT-4o) and V18 (Llama) each have their own correct cells, the natural idea is to let different models vote. V20 to V28 tried eight versions to find the most effective voting mechanism.

Version What changed Train
V20 Raise screenshot zoom from 3.5x to 4.5x, hoping vision sees more clearly 83.6%
V21 Re-crop the Package Outline block for the dimension columns and send it to the LLM for a second extraction 84.5%
V22 Swap PDF extractor: use the TOC (table of contents) as a base to find the Package Outline page 84.5%
V23 Three-model voting: V12 (GPT-4o) + V18 (Llama) + V22 (TOC GPT-4o), take the majority answer 85.5%
V24 Eight-model voting: V10 / V11 / V12 / V14 / V15 / V17 / V18 / V22 all vote, take the majority answer 84.5%
V25 cluster-aware vote: cluster the versions first, then vote 84.5%
V26 Three-axis Llama-consistent override: for the L / W / H columns, when V18 (Llama) differs from every other model → take V18's answer 88.2%
V27 narrow re-extract as the 4th vote: for cells where V12 / V18 / V22 disagree, re-extract with a cropped image + narrow prompt, and combine with the original 3 votes into a 4-way majority 88.2%
V28 divergence override: for L / W / H cells where V12 = V18 = V22 all three agree but narrow vs baseline error > 30%, take the narrow answer 87.3%

Two key findings:

  • V24 eight-model voting regressed — six of the eight versions are GPT-4o variants (V12 / V14 / V15 / V17 / V21 / V22), and their wrong positions overlap heavily. Stuffing them all into the voting pool means six consistent errors override V18's (Llama) two correct votes. What multi-model voting counts is "how many independent architectures there are", and how many versions you ran does not count.
  • V26 three-axis Llama-consistent override is the biggest breakthrough of the multi-model voting stage — +3 cells. Looking at CD4148WTP (SOD-523, body 1.65 × 0.9 mm), V12 and V22 (both GPT-4o family) got all three L / W / H axes wrong, and V18 (Llama) got all three right. Trigger condition: "when V18 differs from the other models on all three L, W, H axes" use V18's answer. This rule hardcodes no part number and applies the same judgment to any PDF.

# V29 — V36: prompt rewrite + override mix, reaching 89.1%

After V26 pushed to 88.2%, among the remaining errors the I_F column of 1N4148W stood out — the answer key is 0.15 (I_O, average rectified current), but V12 kept extracting 0.3 (I_F, peak forward current). V29 rewrote the prompt to raise I_O's priority ahead of I_F, but this change unexpectedly affected other fields, and it deducted more than it added. V31 solves this with a "mix".

Version What changed Train
V29 prompt rewrite: I_O takes priority over I_F, Tj fallback rule added 85.5%
V30 V29 prompt + V26 voting logic 87.3%
V31 2-field-only override: V26 as the base, but use V29's answer for the two columns I_O / I_F and Min Operating Temperature 89.1%
V32 V31 + Tj regex post-processing 89.1%
V33 V31 + LLM re-query the Tj column 88.2%
V34 V31 + verify-all critic (let the LLM verify all fields itself) 89.1%
V35 4-way voting adds V29 88.2%
V36 V31 + Llama critic (swap the critic model) 88.2%

The spirit of V31: "apply a given rule only where a given field truly benefits". The V29 prompt is right for I_O / I_F, but it hurts L / W / H. The V31 solution is "apply V29 only to the two columns that benefit, keep V26 for the rest" — I kept it local and let go of replacing the whole prompt with V29.

V32 to V36 all tried to push higher: regex for Tj, LLM re-query Tj, verify-all critic, Llama critic — none of them got past V31. The reason is that the specbook's reading rule for the "Tj has only a single value" case is complex, where the same Tj single value maps to a different specbook column for different parts (some map to Tj, some to Tamb low, some to Tstg low). Any fixed trigger gets some parts right and others wrong, and the net value will not be positive.

# V37 — V44: schema rules enter, reaching 95.5%

V37: L = max(D, E) swap rule

Observing the specbook train 10 PDFs, every ground truth is L ≥ W (package length is greater than or equal to width).

Rule: when the (L, W) the LLM extracts satisfies W > L, force-swap the two values.

This rescued DFLS160's L / W swap and the MSB30M W correction, train +3 cells, pushing from 88.2% to 91.8%.

V38 — V40: L / W candidate query

Observing parts like BAS16 and BAS21H: the standard answer takes the lead variant (E1, the full length including the pins, and H_E, the lead-to-lead span) as the dimension, skipping the package body's D / E. V38 / V39 / V40 all try "let the LLM run a narrow query on the cropped image to find the max of all candidates on that axis".

Version What changed Train
V38 W H_E override: the trigger condition is too wide, mis-firing on 1N4148W 89.1%
V39 L_outer override: run an LLM query on the L axis to find the max candidate, ratio 1.05 — 1.5 trigger 92.7%
V40 L + W dual-axis combined: V39 plus a W-axis H_E override, ratio 1.4 — 1.8 strictly excludes 1N4148W 94.5%

The key design of V40: the ratio upper bound is deliberately locked at 1.8. Part 1N4148W has an H_E / E ratio of 2.09, which exceeds 1.8 and so does not trigger, avoiding turning its correct answer wrong. This ratio boundary is a physical convention derived from observing "which parts should apply, which should not" (a lead much larger than the body means another package design), and triggering automatically on this general principle covers parts like 1N4148W.

V41 — V43: failed Tj rule attempts

After 94.5%, the only remaining eyesore is BAV99W's single Min Operating Temperature field. BAV99W's datasheet lists only a single Tj value, with no Tamb or Tstg range. But BAS16 and CD4148WTP are also "Tj single only" — for these three parts the specbook uses a different column for Min Operating Temperature (BAV99W takes the Tj single, BAS16 takes the Tamb range low, CD4148WTP takes the Tstg range low).

Version What changed Train
V41 LLM judges Tj single + whether a Tamb exists itself: the LLM misjudged BAS16's "Ta=25 condition" as a Tamb range, so it did not trigger to rescue BAV99W 94.5%
V42 regex for the Tamb range: PyMuPDF column cannot extract the multi-line Tamb, judges no Tamb across the batch → BAS16 / CD4148WTP mis-fire -2 93.6%
V43 Same as V40 but H_E ratio upper bound tightened to 1.8: the LLM gives BAV99W H_E=2.4, ratio 1.92, which gets excluded 94.5%

V44: Tj single + grep "Tamb" literal, reaching 95.5%

V41 failed at "the LLM judging whether a Tamb exists itself" — the LLM is too clever and counts "Ta=25", a measurement condition, into Tamb. V42 failed at "pure regex for the Tamb range" — PyMuPDF extracting a multi-line table drops characters. V44 changed it so that two conditions must hold at the same time to trigger:

  • Condition 1 — the LLM judges whether Tj is a single value (semantic judgment)
  • Condition 2 — Python regex greps whether the PDF text literally contains the string "Tamb" or "Ambient Temperature" (literal judgment)

BAV99W's datasheet has no "Tamb" literal string, while BAS16 and CD4148WTP both do. The intersection of the two conditions distinguishes them precisely. V44 pushed from 94.5% to 95.5%, 105 of 110 cells correct.

Why this section is the hardest

V44 is the most refined rule in the whole post. For the same three parts that are "Tj single value", the specbook uses three different columns, and the only generic distinguishing point falls on "whether the PDF text contains the four characters Tamb", something that at first glance does not look like an ML feature. Writing one rule is easy; finding the correct trigger boundary is the hard part — this lesson applies to any data extraction task with an inconsistent schema.

# V45 — V48: final tuning

After V44 reached 95.5%, five train cells remained unrescuable. V45 to V48 tried several tuning directions to see whether one more cell could be pushed, and this score-chasing line finally stopped at V47 (train 95.5%).

Version What changed Train
V45 Re-extract with wide raster for parts with anomalous W / H ratios 95.5%
V46 Use Llama to re-extract parts with anomalous W / H: it changed BAV99W's W wrong -1 94.5%
V47 Llama pure-swap detection: trigger swap only when Llama's D ≈ baseline W AND Llama's E ≈ baseline L 95.5%
V48 Run multi-sample on H_E and take the median 93.6%

V47 is the dual of V44 — V37's swap rule can mis-fire on certain package types (the "rule coverage vs always correct" tradeoff mentioned in the V37 section). V47 adds a layer of "trigger only when Llama's dimensions are a fully pure swap pattern against the baseline" as a stricter second verification, tightening the swap rule from "swap if W > L" to "swap only if W > L and Llama confirms a pure swap". The final result is still train 95.5%, with the 5 unrescuable cells unchanged.

# After pushing to 95.5%, I re-checked and confirmed: from V37 on it is actually overfit

Up to this point I described those rules from V37 to V44 as "generic" — ratio thresholds, text patterns, no hardcoded part numbers. Before submitting the assignment I stopped and re-audited once, and the judgment flipped: these rules overfit "this particular scoring set".

The key is "the scoring set has only 10 datasheets"

This assignment has no independent test set, and what gets scored is those 10 datasheets. V40's ratio upper bound "deliberately locked at 1.8" exists so 1N4148W does not get changed wrong, and V44's "grep Tamb" exists to precisely distinguish BAV99W from BAS16 — these threshold values were tuned by me "looking at which cells among those 10 parts are wrong and adjusting back". The trigger conditions look generic in form (ratios, strings), but the source of the thresholds is the scoring set itself. Swap in a fresh batch of datasheets and these thresholds are not guaranteed to remain optimal. That is overfit to the scoring set.

Rejudging by this stricter standard, the clean ceiling sits at V23 three-model voting (around 85.5%), plus generic rules that do not rely on per-part back-reasoning, about up to V31 (89.1%). Every cell of improvement after V37 carries the property "the threshold was tuned by looking at the answers". Chasing a high score and staying generic is one tradeoff here, and I chose to hold the generic line.

# The 5 unrescuable cells: schema the rules / extraction have not caught yet

After V47, train still has 5 cells unrescued. Checking each cell's state part by part:

Part Field Extracted value Answer key Schema not caught yet
BAV99W V_RRM 100 150 A 150V annotation somewhere in the datasheet that the current extraction did not scan
BAV99W W 1.25 2.2 Whether the specbook uses H_E or lead-tip-to-tip, the LLM did not catch the correct sample
BAV99W H 1.1 1 On the edge of the 10% tolerance, needs a finer dimension reference
MBR15U150 W 5.5 4.1 Variant dimension table inside a multi-product datasheet, the prompt did not point to the correct variant
MBR15U150 H 4.1 1.2 Same multi-product variant selection problem as above

These 5 cells are all "the specbook used some specific reading rule, and the current prompt / extraction has not caught that rule yet". There are two paths to rescue them:

  • Path A — write a part-specific override: write a rule directly in the code like "for BAV99W force V_RRM = 150". One cell +1, but this is the definition of overfitting the train set — useless for any unseen PDF.
  • Path B — find a generic rule that is correct for all 5 parts at once: for example "for a multi-product datasheet force selecting a certain variant" or "for the BAV99W series take V_RRM from a certain note". But every generic rule I tried either trips on other parts (trigger too wide) or misses this part (trigger too narrow).

The existence of these 5 cells is itself a signal: pushing the score to 95.5% has to rely on "tuning thresholds by looking at these 10 parts", and that is exactly overfit. The remaining 5 cells correspond to "the specbook still has reading rules not covered by any generic pattern" — a schema-level limitation that any generic schema extraction system runs into.

# Final submission: the integrated version I confirmed zero-overfit three times over

Removing all the score-chasing rules from V37 onward that "tune thresholds by looking at the answers", the final submission is an integrated version built only from generic components:

  • Three-model voting (V23) — GPT-4o multimodal + Llama-4 vision + GPT-4o (TOC variant), take the majority value column by column
  • Generic cropped dimension table — locate the dimension table by searching for the "Package Outline / Mechanical Dimensions" anchor text, then high-resolution rasterize that block and send it to vision
  • Generic physical rules — take length/width to the outermost edge including the pins, take the Max column, convert tolerances. Name no part, hardcode no value
Scoring basis note (the two numbers use different bases, do not compare directly)

The submitted report switched to the 100-cell basis: the assignment has 11 columns × 10 items = 110 cells, of which the Part Number is the input given by the assignment (known before running) and is not counted; subtract it and there are 100 cells. So the report headline reads 91 cells out of 100 (91%). The earlier numbers in this post from V2 76.4% to V47 95.5% are train accuracy on the 110-cell basis (including the Part Number).
